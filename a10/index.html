AI economics and pricing

How to know what an AI feature costs, make it cheaper without making it worse, and price it so heavy users don't sink it. Then build a cost model and pull the levers.

The situation

Ledgerly launched "unlimited AI drafts" on every plan. Three months later, finance shares a chart: AI costs are growing twice as fast as revenue, and 2% of users cause half the bill.

"Tokens cost fractions of a cent!" "Fractions of a cent, times 40 calls a task, times a million tasks."

1Token maths and cost per taskEvery AI feature has a unit cost. Know it before you price it.9 min

The idea

Classic software costs almost nothing per extra use. AI features don't: every request pays for tokens (A1), priced per million, with separate prices for input (what you send) and output (what the model writes). Output usually costs several times more than input.

The number that matters for a PM isn't the price per token. It's the cost per task: what it costs to do one useful thing for one user.

The framework

Cost per task

cost per call = input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price
cost per task = cost per call × calls per task (+ retries, retrieval, tools)
cost per user per month = cost per task × tasks per user
  • Calls per task is where costs hide: agents (A6) and retries can turn one task into 20 calls.
  • Look at the spread, not the average. Your heaviest 5% of users can cost more than everyone else together.
  • Measure real token counts from your traces (A8). Estimates from demos are usually too low.

Real example

Model choice moves cost more than anything else. On Google's Gemini API price list (checked October 2026), Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite cost $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.40 per million output tokens; Gemini 3.1 Pro cost $2 and $12. For a call with 2,000 input and 500 output tokens, that's about $0.0004 against $0.01: a 25-fold difference for the same request.

Source: Google, Gemini API pricing. Prices change often: always check the current page.

Where it breaks

  • Prices fall fast, but usage often grows faster, especially with agents and long contexts.
  • "It's fractions of a cent" stops being true at millions of requests, or when one task loops.
  • Hidden costs: embeddings, vector storage, evals, monitoring and human review also cost money.

2The cost levers: routing, caching, batching, trimmingCut cost without cutting quality, and keep latency in view.9 min

The idea

Most AI cost savings come from five levers. Use them in roughly this order, and check every change against your evals (A7) so quality doesn't quietly slip.

The framework

LeverHow it worksTypical effect
RouteCheap model first; escalate hard or uncertain cases to a strong oneOften the biggest saving
CacheReuse a long, identical prompt prefix (instructions, documents)Cached input at a fraction of the price
BatchSend non-urgent jobs to a batch API; results within 24 hoursAbout 50% off at major providers
TrimShorter outputs, only the context the task needsCheaper and faster
CapLimits on steps, tokens and spend per task and per userProtects you from the tail

Latency is part of the deal. Bigger models and longer outputs are slower. Measure time to first token and total time, and stream output so people see progress (A9).

Real example

Anthropic's price list shows how caching works. Writing a prompt prefix to the 5-minute cache costs 1.25 times the normal input price; reading it back costs 0.1 times on most models. So a cached prefix pays for itself after a single reuse, and a help assistant that sends the same long guide on every call pays about a tenth for that part of the input. The Batch API halves prices for jobs that can wait, and the two discounts stack.

Source: Anthropic, Claude pricing and prompt caching docs, checked November 2026.

Where it breaks

  • Routing without evals saves money and quietly loses quality. Test the cheap path on your golden set.
  • Caching needs a stable prefix. Put fixed content first; anything that changes early in the prompt breaks the cache.
  • Over-trimming context brings back hallucinations (A8). Trim what's irrelevant, not what's needed.

3Pricing AI featuresSeats, usage, outcomes or bundled: make price follow value, and protect your margin.9 min

The idea

AI breaks the classic SaaS assumption that one more user costs almost nothing. So pricing AI features is partly a margin question: will your heaviest users cost more than they pay?

The framework

ModelFits whenWatch out for
Bundled in the planLight, predictable use; AI as a reason to upgradeHeavy users eat the margin
Allowance + top-up (credits)Use varies a lot between usersMust be easy to understand and track
Usage-basedDevelopers and power users who accept meteringBill shock; hard to budget
Outcome-basedThe AI completes a clear unit of workArguments about what counts as done

Three checks before launch: gross margin at the median user, the cost of your top 5% of users, and what users see before they hit a limit.

Real example

Intercom prices its Fin AI agent per outcome: $0.99 for each outcome, mainly a conversation Fin resolves, charged at most once per conversation, on top of Intercom's seat plans. Customers pay when the AI does the work, not for a seat it doesn't use. The trade-off is that the definition of "resolved" carries real weight, and Intercom publishes it in its help centre.

Source: Fin pricing and Intercom's help article on Fin outcomes, checked 2026.

Where it breaks

  • "Unlimited" invites exactly the users who cost you most. If there's a limit, say so up front.
  • Changing what a price buys without clear notice breaks trust faster than a price increase.
  • Pricing at today's token costs ignores that both costs and usage will change. Review it every quarter.

Cases

A company that priced its AI by the work it finishes, and one that changed what its price bought without telling users clearly.

Got it right

Intercom's Fin: priced per outcome

Intercom charges $0.99 per Fin outcome, mainly a conversation the AI agent resolves, billed at most once per conversation, and publishes what counts as an outcome. The price follows the value customers get, and Intercom carries the cost risk of each conversation, which pushes it to keep cost per resolution well below the price (fin.ai/pricing, checked 2026).

Price the unit of work customers care about, define it clearly, and make sure your cost per unit leaves room for a margin.

Got it wrong

Cursor's June 2025 pricing change

On 16 June 2025, Cursor switched its $20-a-month Pro plan from a set number of requests to $20 of frontier-model usage at API prices, plus unlimited use of its Auto mode. Many users had read the change as "unlimited" and were surprised by charges. On 4 July, Cursor apologised for the unclear rollout, refunded unexpected usage between 16 June and 4 July, and added usage dashboards and clearer docs (Cursor blog, "Clarifying our pricing"; TechCrunch, 7 July 2025).

When AI costs force a pricing change, the change itself may be right, but users need to see clearly what they get, and a meter before the bill.

Show a worked example

Feature: Ledgerly's AI help assistant. Cost per task: about 4,000 input tokens (mostly the help guide) and 150 output tokens. On a small model, with the guide cached, about $0.0003 per answer; one in ten escalated to a strong model brings the average to about $0.001.

Per user: 40 questions a month, so about $0.04. Levers: cache the guide, route 90% to the small model (checked on the golden set), answers capped at 150 words.

Pricing: bundled in all plans, because the cost is tiny and it reduces support tickets. Protection: 500 questions a month, with a friendly notice at 400; a step cap per answer. Review: monthly cost dashboard, and a review if cost per user doubles or margin on the plan drops below 80%.

Key takeaways

  1. Know your cost per task. Input and output tokens, times calls per task, times tasks per user. Measure it from traces.
  2. Pull the levers in order. Route to the cheapest model that passes your evals, cache what repeats, batch what can wait, trim what nobody reads.
  3. Watch the tail. Heavy users and looping agents decide your margin. Cap steps and spend per task.
  4. Price for value, without surprises. Bundled, allowance, usage or outcome: say clearly what's included, and show a meter.
